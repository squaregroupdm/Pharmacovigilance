Understanding PV

safer medicines

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), Pharmacovigilance is defined as the science and activities relating to the detection, assessment, understanding, and prevention of adverse effects or any other medicine-related problem.

The primary goals of pharmacovigilance are to:

  • Enhance patient care and patient safety in relation to the use of medicines;
  • Support public health programmes by providing reliable and balanced information on the risk-benefit profile of medicines;
  • Promote the rational, safe, and effective use of medicines; and
  • Encourage a culture of transparent and timely safety reporting among patients and healthcare professionals.

No clinical trial can uncover every possible side effect in diverse patient populations under real-world conditions. Spontaneous reporting by patients, caregivers, doctors, pharmacists, and nurses plays a vital role in post-market surveillance. By sharing your experience, you help us:

  • Detect new, rare, or unexpected side effects early.
  • Identify patient-specific risk factors or interactions.
  • Update product information, safety labeling, and educational materials.
  • Contribute to national and global pharmacovigilance databases to protect public health.

We encourage patients, caregivers, healthcare professionals (physicians, pharmacists, nurses), and any member of the public to report any suspected adverse event, adverse drug reaction, or product quality complaint associated with the use of Square's products (manufactured, marketed, and/or distributed by Square Pharmaceuticals PLC).

A complete adverse event report should ideally contain four minimum data elements, referred to as PREP. See the PREP checklist in the reporting section.

Key Terms You Should Know

What should be reported

To ensure transparent reporting, it is important to understand what safety events should be reported to us.

01 / 05
Term 01

Adverse Event (AE)

An adverse event is any event associated with the use of a drug or drug like substance, which has nothing to do with the treatment of the disease.

Usually poor quality of medicine, inadequate or excessive use of medicine which causes adverse reactions in the human body; all are included in Adverse Drug Event (ADE).

Term 02

Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR)

The Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) is the unexpected reaction that takes place in the human body either due to the use of drug or drug like substance.

This may happen even if someone has taken the medicine in indicated doses normally used for prophylaxis, diagnosis, or therapy. If this occurs, one must report Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR).

Term 03

Serious Adverse Event

An event resulting in death, a life-threatening condition, hospitalisation or prolongation of hospitalisation, persistent or significant disability, a congenital anomaly, or another medically important condition.

Term 04

Product Quality Complaint (PQC)

Any complaint relating to the physical or chemical quality of a product (e.g., packaging defect, discolouration, foreign particle).

Term 05

Special Situations

Reports regarding medication errors, product misuse, abuse, overdose, occupational exposure, lack of therapeutic efficacy, or suspected transmission of infectious agents.

What a complete report contains

Four minimum data elements, remembered as PREP

Even if you are unsure about all of the details, please report. Partial information is still valuable and can be followed up by our Pharmacovigilance team.

P
Patient details

Initials/name, age or age group, gender, pregnancy status (if applicable)

R
Reporter details

Name, contact number/e-mail, occupation (e.g., patient, physician, pharmacist)

E
Event details

Description of the reaction/event, date of onset, severity, seriousness, outcome

P
Product details

Brand name, dose, batch/lot number, route of administration, other concomitant medicines

Governance

How our system
is built

Led by the designated Qualified Person for Pharmacovigilance (QPPV).

The Pharmacovigilance function at Square Pharmaceuticals PLC is led by the designated Qualified Person for Pharmacovigilance (QPPV) and is responsible for:

  • Receiving, documenting, and processing Individual Case Safety Reports (ICSRs) for Square's products, using standardized medical terminology (MedDRA) for consistent coding of adverse events;
  • Assessing causality, seriousness, and expectedness of reported events;
  • Timely reporting of adverse events to the Directorate General of Drug Administration (DGDA) in accordance with applicable regulatory timelines;
  • Continuous monitoring of the safety profile of marketed products through signal detection, trend analysis, and periodic safety review of aggregate data;
  • Preparing periodic safety update reports and, where applicable, risk management plans for products requiring enhanced monitoring;
  • Conducting pharmacovigilance training and awareness programmes for employees, healthcare professionals, and other stakeholders; and
  • Maintaining a Pharmacovigilance Quality Management System (PV-QMS) aligned with national requirements and internationally recognized good pharmacovigilance practice (GVP) principles.

Pharmacovigilance activities in Bangladesh are governed by the Directorate General of Drug Administration (DGDA), which operates the national Adverse Drug Reaction Monitoring (ADRM) Cell under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. Bangladesh, through the DGDA, is a member of the WHO Programme for International Drug Monitoring. Square Pharmaceuticals PLC operates its pharmacovigilance system in compliance with these national requirements, while drawing on internationally harmonized guidelines to align with global good practice.

Square Pharmaceuticals PLC's pharmacovigilance system is designed with reference to internationally recognized frameworks and guidelines that shape modern drug-safety practice worldwide, including:

WHOProgramme for International Drug Monitoring, coordinated by the Uppsala Monitoring Centre (UMC) in Sweden, which maintains the global ICSR database (VigiBase).
ICHE2A (Clinical Safety Data Management), E2B (Data Elements for Individual Case Safety Reports), E2C/E2C(R2) (Periodic Benefit-Risk Evaluation Report), E2D (Post-Approval Safety Data Management), E2E (Pharmacovigilance Planning), and E2F (Development Safety Update Report), which together define globally harmonized standards for safety data collection, evaluation, and reporting.
CIOMSCouncil for International Organizations of Medical Sciences, whose working group reports underpin many current practices in aggregate safety reporting, signal management, and benefit-risk assessment.
ISoPInternational Society of Pharmacovigilance, a professional scientific body that promotes pharmacovigilance education, best practice, and the safe use of medicines globally.
GVP & MedDRAGood Pharmacovigilance Practices principles and the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities, used for structured, standardized coding and classification of adverse events.
